### 🔥 The Big Stories

**Nvidia Signs $6B Deal with Poolside to Build Open-Source Nemotron**

The US just made its biggest open-source AI bet yet. Nvidia has signed a $6 billion deal with Poolside to accelerate development of their open-source Nemotron model, signaling that the chip giant isn't content sitting at the infrastructure layer while the model wars play out above them. This is a meaningful strategic move — Nvidia is essentially funding a serious competitor to Meta's Llama lineage, with the full weight of their hardware roadmap behind it.

Why it matters: The open-source frontier has been largely a Meta vs. Chinese labs story for the past year. Nvidia stepping in with real capital changes the incentive structure. Poolside gets compute access that no other lab can match; Nvidia gets a flagship open model optimized for their silicon. Watch for this to compress the closed-vs-open performance gap faster than most expect.

**Anthropic's Claude Struggles to Convert Despite Revenue Surge**

A bruising [FT piece](https://www.ft.com/content/5ee49718-c258-4f01-aa32-7e5b76ae5245) is getting traction on HN (589 pts, 527 comments) today: Anthropic's best model is struggling to attract users even as cheaper alternatives thrive. This lands the same week that [one commenter on X](https://x.com/RealNickMugalli/status/2091842917333610950) is claiming Anthropic's Q2 revenue hit $11.5B — up 144% QoQ — and projecting a $100B ARR run rate by year-end. These two things aren't necessarily contradictory: enterprise API spend is booming, but consumer and developer mindshare is a different game. Claude may be winning the B2B quiet war while losing the culture war. The HN thread is worth reading for the nuance.

Also fresh today: Anthropic is [reportedly asking job candidates a blunt question about money](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/24/scoop-anthropic-candidates-face-blunt-money-question) during interviews — context unclear, but signals something about internal culture and compensation strategy shifting.
