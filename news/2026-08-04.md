### 🔥 The Big Stories

**Google's About to Make Everyone Look Foolish Again**

According to [a widely-shared post this morning](https://x.com/bridgemindai/status/2084599477705248822), Gemini 3.5 Pro drops next week — and the pattern here is worth taking seriously. Google goes quiet, everyone writes the obituary, then they ship something that resets the leaderboard. Gemini 3.1 Pro, Gemini 2.0 Flash, the Gemini Ultra refresh — all followed the same arc. Six months of silence, one interim Flash update, and a comeback nobody was ready for. Meanwhile, [Austen nailed the underlying problem](https://x.com/Austen/status/2084403698000601553): Google had every structural advantage — researchers, data, compute, distribution — and still got lapped culturally. The real question for 3.5 Pro isn't benchmarks. It's whether Google has fixed the culture that made them slow in the first place. Watch the developer reception closely, not the press release.

**Paul Graham Puts Greptile on the Map**

PG publicly named [Greptile](https://x.com/paulg/status/2084413436679836037) as his example of a company that understood product-market fit as a margin of safety — and gave founders permission to share it. For the uninitiated: Greptile builds AI-powered codebase understanding, letting teams ask natural language questions about their own repos. Getting named by Graham in this context isn't just a pat on the back; it's a signal to investors and enterprise buyers alike. One of the better YC company names out there, as pg also notes. Worth a look if you're in the dev tooling space.

### 💡 Builder's Corner

**Graph Engineering for Better LLM Context**

[Greg Isenberg's breakdown](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/2084359492569907643) on "graph engineering" is worth 15 minutes of your time. The core idea: instead of dumping raw docs into vector RAG (which fails on global/relational questions), you extract entities and relationships first, cluster them, then feed structured graph context to your model. A [Microsoft engineer's deep dive](https://x.com/0xMorlex/status/2084616172960374870) walks through the implementation. Relevant for anyone building Claude Code or Codex workflows on top of large private codebases.

**Stanford Released a Free Course on Self-Improving Agents**

[Stanford's course on self-improving AI agents](https://x.com/4rblaber/status/2084604787849326839) just dropped publicly — covers feedback loops, memory, and how agents learn from experience. Free. If you're building anything agentic and haven't thought about how your system gets better over time without retraining, start here.
