OpenAI Launches "Daybreak" Cybersecurity Initiative

The AI security arms race just accelerated — and OpenAI wants to be your ally, not your enemy.

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**OpenAI Goes All-In on Cybersecurity Defense**

Sam Altman [announced](https://x.com/sama/status/2053951874408276193) OpenAI's new "Daybreak" initiative yesterday — a comprehensive effort to help companies defend against AI-powered cyber attacks. The timing isn't coincidental: as AI gets "super good at cybersecurity," OpenAI wants to ensure the good guys have the best tools first.

This comes alongside the launch of OpenAI's [Deployment Company](https://x.com/gdb/), focused on helping businesses maximize their AI implementations. The message is clear: OpenAI isn't just selling models anymore — they're building an enterprise services empire around AI adoption and security.

**Google Confirms: Criminal Hackers Now Using AI to Find Zero-Days**

The theoretical became real. Google [reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/11/us/politics/google-hackers-attack-ai.html) that criminal hackers successfully used AI to discover a major software vulnerability — the first confirmed case of AI-assisted zero-day discovery in the wild. This validates what security researchers have been warning about for months: the attack/defense balance is shifting rapidly.

**Circle Unveils AI Agent Payment Infrastructure**

Circle [launched](https://investor.circle.com/news/news-details/2026/Circle-Launches-AI-Infrastructure-to-Power-the-Agentic-Economy/default.aspx) its "Circle Agent Stack" — purpose-built payment rails for AI agents to transact autonomously. Think Stripe for robots. The first product handles programmable spend controls and real Visa/Mastercard integration, solving the "how do agents actually buy things" problem that's been blocking agent deployment at scale.

-   **Former Wiz Execs Raise $50M:** Cybersecurity startup Frame [secured major funding](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/s1idmmtabx) to tackle AI-powered social engineering attacks, which they say are "dramatically easier to create and much harder to detect."
