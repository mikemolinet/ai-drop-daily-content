The AI infrastructure play is heating up. Sam Altman just announced OpenAI will invest $2 million in tokens into every startup in the current Y Combinator batch — a move that's part capacity planning, part ecosystem lock-in, and part bet on the "tokenmaxxing" startup wave.

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**OpenAI Goes All-In on YC with $2M Token Deals**

Sam Altman dropped the news yesterday that [OpenAI is offering $2M in API credits to every YC startup](https://x.com/sama/status/2056933166875857290) in exchange for equity. The program runs until OpenAI sells out of their current allocation, with plans to repeat it as they scale compute capacity.

This isn't just generosity — it's strategic infrastructure building. Altman noted that customers are "increasingly asking for certainty on capacity" and that the world will be "capacity-constrained for some time." By getting startups hooked on OpenAI tokens early, they're building demand predictability while locking in the next generation of AI-native companies. The timing coincides with OpenAI offering [discounted tokens for 1-3 year commits](https://x.com/sama/status/2056827105401614656) to enterprise customers.

What's fascinating is Altman's framing around "tokenmaxxing startups" — companies that will work internally using tokens and build token-native products. This suggests OpenAI sees a future where computational credits become a core business asset, not just an operational expense.

**Google Pushes Back with Gemini 3.5 Flash at I/O**

Google responded to the AI race with [Gemini 3.5 Flash](https://x.com/simonw/status/2056867815605625172) at their I/O event, though early reactions are mixed. Simon Willison noted it's "3x the price of Gemini 3 Flash" and Google plans to use it across their own products. However, some developers are calling it "barely different from the previous Flash" with questionable cost-effectiveness compared to the modest improvements.

The lukewarm reception highlights Google's challenge: they're iterating incrementally while OpenAI makes bold infrastructure plays and Anthropic ships developer-focused tools. Google's also launching [Gemini Spark personal AI agents](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/google-ai-ultra-gemini-spark-omni.html) in beta for Google AI Ultra subscribers, but the real test will be whether they can match the execution velocity of their competitors.
