### 🔥 The Big Stories

**Sakana AI Launches Fugu — A Model Trained to Command Other Models**

Tokyo-based Sakana AI [dropped Fugu](https://x.com/BullTheoryio/status/2069003954919297400), a new model specifically architected to orchestrate other models — and early benchmarks show it matching Claude Fable 5 and Mythos performance. That's a significant claim. Sakana was co-founded by researchers including one of the original Transformer paper authors, so these aren't newcomers fumbling with fine-tuning.

What makes Fugu interesting isn't just the benchmark score — it's the design philosophy. Training a model specifically to command other models is a bet that orchestration intelligence is its own distinct capability, separate from raw reasoning or knowledge. If that framing holds, Fugu could become infrastructure for multi-agent systems rather than a competitor to frontier chat models. Watch whether devs actually reach for it in agent loops.

The broader signal: Japan is now a legitimate player in frontier AI, not just a consumer of it.

**Claude Identity Verification — 799 Upvotes and 666 Comments on HN**

Anthropic's [identity verification feature for Claude](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14328960-identity-verification-on-claude) is generating serious debate on Hacker News — nearly 700 comments, which is HN-scale controversy. The core tension: Anthropic wants to unlock more capable behavior for verified users, but the community is deeply skeptical about handing over identity information to an AI company.

This connects directly to the ongoing Fable 5 access saga. Anthropic has been threading a needle between capability and access control since the government suspension earlier this month. Identity verification is their answer — but it raises obvious questions about what data gets stored, how it's used, and whether this creates a two-tiered Claude experience that disadvantages privacy-conscious developers. A [related HN thread](https://www.marble.onl/posts/cancel_claude.html) arguing there's "minimal downside to switching to open models" hit 269 points — the two threads together paint a picture of genuine user trust erosion.

### 📡 Quick Hits

**Codex Logging Bug Is Eating Your SSD.** A [GitHub issue](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/28224) with 176 points and 91 comments reveals Codex has a logging bug that can write terabytes to local drives. If you're running Codex locally, check your disk usage _now_. This is the kind of silent killer that shows up as "my laptop just died" three weeks from now.

**Apertus: Open Foundation Model for Sovereign AI.** A new open foundation model called [Apertus](https://apertvs.ai/) just landed on HN with 442 points — the top score in today's feed. Framed around "sovereign AI," it's aimed at organizations that can't or won't route data through US-based model providers. Worth bookmarking if you work with European or government clients.

**Anthropic Hiring for Claude Code.** [Fiona Fung](https://x.com/lennysan/status/2068713369398567187), who leads Claude Code and Cowork at Anthropic (11 years at Microsoft on Visual Studio/TypeScript, then Meta), is [actively hiring](https://x.com/lennysan/status/2068778340090343931) two profiles: creative builders with product sense, and deep systems experts. If that's you, the door is open.

**Garry Tan Open-Sourced GBrain.** YC's president [dropped a reference](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2068701357696323769) to open-sourcing GBrain, his personal context/memory layer. His argument: AGI gives you intelligence, but you still need to curate your own context to get the real unlock. Practical framing for founders building personal AI infrastructure.

**Paul Graham on AI-Written Emails.** PG [complained](https://x.com/paulg/status/2068994868412629467) that AI-written emails are longer than human ones and full of "plausible-sounding text." Not news exactly — but if you're cold-emailing founders or investors, this is a useful calibration. Shorter wins.
