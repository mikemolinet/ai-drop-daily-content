OpenAI is rolling out its first domain-specific frontier model to cybersecurity defenders, while the White House blocks Anthropic's competing security model from broader release.

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**OpenAI Goes Cybersecurity-First with GPT-5.5-Cyber**

Sam Altman [announced yesterday](https://x.com/sama/status/2049712078836170843) that OpenAI is beginning rollout of GPT-5.5-Cyber, a specialized frontier model designed for cybersecurity work. The model is initially going to "critical cyber defenders" with plans to "work with the entire ecosystem and the government to figure out trusted access."

This marks OpenAI's first domain-specific frontier model release — a shift from their usual general-purpose approach. The cybersecurity focus isn't coincidental: as AI capabilities advance, the dual-use problem becomes acute. A model that can defend systems can also attack them. OpenAI appears to be betting that controlled release to defenders first creates a security advantage before broader deployment.

**White House Blocks Anthropic's Security Model Access**

Meanwhile, the Biden administration has [reportedly blocked](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2049783340715454968) Anthropic's push to expand access to "Mythos," their powerful model that can find and exploit software vulnerabilities. According to WSJ reporting, the model's capabilities are "serious enough to trigger national-security controls."

The contrast is striking: OpenAI gets government cooperation for cybersecurity model rollout while Anthropic faces restrictions. This suggests OpenAI's "work with government" approach is paying dividends in the high-stakes world of dual-use AI capabilities. The race isn't just about who builds the best models anymore — it's about who can navigate the regulatory landscape.

**Microsoft Study: AI Agents Damage 25% of Documents During Long Edits**

New Microsoft research shows current AI assistants [corrupt about 25% of document content](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2049779289592139929) during extended editing sessions, with many models performing far worse. This isn't just a quality issue — it's a fundamental problem with how we're deploying AI for knowledge work.

The study highlights the gap between demo-perfect AI and real-world reliability. As companies rush to deploy AI agents for document workflows, this research suggests we're not ready for unsupervised delegation of complex editing tasks.

### 📡 Quick Hits

**Anthropic's Claude Solves Expert-Level Biology Problems**: Claude Mythos Preview [solved 29.6% of bioinformatics problems](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2049781967248568341) that human experts couldn't crack, suggesting AI is starting to push the boundaries of scientific discovery.

**IBM's Granite 4.1 Matches Much Larger Models**: IBM's new 8B parameter model [performs comparably to 32B MoE models](https://firethering.com/granite-4-1-ibm-open-source-model-family/), proving that smart architecture can beat brute force scaling.

**Zig Project Takes Hard Anti-AI Stance**: The Zig programming language project published their [rationale for rejecting AI-generated contributions](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/zig-anti-ai/), citing concerns about code quality and legal liability.

**Mozilla Opposes Chrome's Prompt API**: Mozilla filed formal opposition to Chrome's built-in AI prompt API, [arguing it creates unfair advantage](https://github.com/mozilla/standards-positions/issues/1213) and privacy concerns.

### 💡 Builder's Corner
