The age of blank-slate AI agents might be ending. While everyone's been arguing about Claude vs GPT, researchers at Hong Kong University of Science and Technology just shipped something more fundamental: [AI agents that actually remember what they've learned](https://x.com/alex_prompter/status/2051245551287689648).

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**AI Agents Get Permanent Memory That Transfers Between Models**

HKUST researchers cracked one of AI's biggest limitations: agents that start every task with zero context. Their new system gives agents persistent memory that improves over time and transfers lessons between different models — no retraining required. Early results show up to 11-point improvements on the hardest benchmarks. This addresses the core problem that every AI agent today is essentially suffering from digital amnesia, forgetting everything it learned the moment a session ends. For builders, this could be the difference between agents that feel like helpful assistants versus actual digital employees.

**Naval's Prediction: "AIs Replace UIs and APIs"**

The [most-engaged tweet](https://x.com/naval/status/2050560057675522500) in our feed today was Naval's four-word thesis that AIs will replace traditional user interfaces and application programming interfaces. While cryptic, it tracks with what we're seeing in the wild — from Anthropic's [rumored org chart where agents run everything](https://x.com/aigoldrushh/status/2051256843297771727) while the CEO sleeps, to developers building products where natural language becomes the primary interface. The shift isn't just about better chatbots; it's about fundamentally different ways software gets built and used.

**OpenAI vs Anthropic: The Monthly Model Musical Chairs**

Developer sentiment is crystallizing around something important: [the AI race has become musical chairs every few months](https://x.com/aaditsh/status/2051235218904801288). One builder noted they use all major LLMs weekly and switch allegiance based on who shipped last. This isn't about fanboy loyalty anymore — it's about developers pragmatically following performance. The current consensus seems split between OpenAI's new Codex features and Anthropic's Claude Code, with many taking a "best tool for the job" approach rather than platform lock-in.
