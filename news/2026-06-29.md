# AI News Drop

**Monday, June 29, 2026**

The story today is who's getting the money and what the frontier looks like when you stop trusting the closed model APIs entirely.

* * *

## 🔥 The Big Stories

**Chamath's 8090 Closes $135M Series A, Led by Salesforce Ventures**

Palihapitiya's enterprise AI company [8090 just announced a $135M Series A](https://x.com/chamath/status/2071571183665881515) led by Salesforce Ventures, with participation from Craft Ventures, WNDR, The Production Board, LAUNCH, and angels including Nikesh Arora and Adam D'Angelo. The deliberate strategy: skip the easy logos, go straight at the hardest buyers — healthcare, insurance, aerospace, life sciences, energy, manufacturing. Regulated industries with high switching costs and real consequences for failure.

This matters because it's a direct bet that enterprise AI is moving past demos and into production in the industries most resistant to it. Salesforce Ventures leading is a signal too — they don't write $135M checks without a clear path to their own ecosystem. Watch for 8090 to become a Salesforce vertical play in disguise.

* * *

**GLM-5.2 Is Challenging Closed Frontier Models — And Devs Are Noticing**

Semgrep published a [detailed benchmark post](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/we-have-mythos-at-home-glm-52-beats-claude-in-our-cyber-benchmarks/) showing GLM-5.2 outperforming Claude on their cybersecurity-specific evals — and it's lighting up Hacker News with 917 points and 421 comments. Separately, one developer [posted that they've flipped their entire AI stack to Chinese models](https://x.com/DeRonin_/status/2071561335234531578) — Kimi K2.7 for reasoning, Qwen 3.7 Max for code — at 87% lower cost with acceptable benchmark gaps.

This is a genuine trend worth tracking. The "Chinese models are cheap but worse" narrative is fraying in real-world workflows. An 18% benchmark gap on code gen is real, but if you're shipping internal tools and the price delta is 7x, the math changes. Expect more teams to quietly run hybrid stacks this quarter.

* * *

**GPT-5.6 "Sol" Blocked — Again**

[VaibhavSisinty's roundup](https://x.com/VaibhavSisinty/status/2071562476353925152) and [AILeaksAndNews](https://x.com/AILeaksAndNews/status/2071563584774017307) are reporting that GPT-5.6 Sol beat Fable 5 on benchmarks but the US government blocked public access — for the second time in two weeks. Ethan Mollick is [asking the obvious question](https://x.com/emollick/status/2071291111352500554): what is OpenAI even saving the GPT-6 label for?

If the government is sitting on two successive frontier releases, the public capability gap versus what exists internally is widening fast. That's a policy story as much as a tech story — and it's one with no clear resolution in sight.
