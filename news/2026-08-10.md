# AI News Drop

**Monday, August 10, 2026**

Google just shelved its most-anticipated model without a public word. Meanwhile, Meta is flooding the zone with open weights. It's a big morning for anyone watching where the frontier actually is.

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**Google Cancels Gemini 3.5 Pro, Pivots to Gemini 4**

It's official enough to matter: Google has quietly shelved Gemini 3.5 Pro, the flagship model it promised for June, according to [reporting from SemiAnalysis](https://x.com/SciTechera/status/2086776603024204087). The model ran into serious trouble with coding and agentic tasks — exactly the benchmarks that matter most right now. Rather than ship something embarrassing, Google appears to be skipping straight to Gemini 4, with Gemini 3.6 Flash potentially serving as a stopgap.

This is notable less for what it says about one model and more for what it says about Google's release discipline. The company that once led on Transformers is now [confirming it still has a shot](https://x.com/Anoyroyc/status/2086750454856491386) — just not on the timeline it promised. Watch whether Gemini 4 actually leapfrogs the current frontier, or whether this becomes another slip. For builders who've been waiting to evaluate Gemini 3.5 Pro for production use: stop waiting.

* * *

**Meta Ships Muse Glimmer — A 30B Open-Weight Agent Model**

Meta dropped [Muse Glimmer](https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-glimmer-open-agentic-model), a 30B open-weight model built specifically for agentic tasks, under Apache 2.0. It's multimodal (text + image), has a quantized version for local deployment, and [CNBC flagged it](https://x.com/CNBC/status/2086791225735086373) as Meta's direct swipe at OpenAI and Anthropic's closed ecosystems.

Zuckerberg paired the release with a [sharp policy argument](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2086775315888849096): any U.S. policy slowing American open-source model releases — even by a month — hands ground to Chinese rivals. That's a calculated PR frame, but it's also not wrong. For local inference builders, a 30B Apache-licensed agentic model that runs on consumer hardware is a genuine unlock. The question is where Glimmer actually lands on coding and tool-use evals versus Llama's latest.

Details are still thin, but if confirmed, it's the kind of incident that moves policy conversations fast.
