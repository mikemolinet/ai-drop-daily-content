**Sierra Scores $950M as AI Enterprise Race Intensifies**

The enterprise AI land grab just got a billion-dollar exclamation point.

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**Sierra raises nearly $1B in massive enterprise AI bet**

Bret Taylor's AI customer service startup [Sierra closed a $950M Series D led by Tiger Global and GV](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/04/sierra-raises-950m-as-the-race-to-own-enterprise-ai-gets-serious/), valuing the company at roughly $4.5B. The round signals serious conviction that AI agents will own enterprise workflows, not just assist with them. Sierra's pitch: replace entire customer service departments with AI that actually resolves issues rather than just routing calls. The timing matters — as foundation models commoditize, the value is shifting to companies that can actually deploy AI at enterprise scale. Taylor's background (former Salesforce co-CEO) gives Sierra credibility with enterprise buyers who've been burned by AI pilot programs that never scaled.

**Suno eyes $5B valuation as AI music goes mainstream**

AI music generator [Suno is closing a Series D that could value it at $5B](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/05/04/ai-music-generator-suno-eyes-5-billion-valuation-after-latest-funding-round/), according to Axios and Billboard. That's a massive jump from its previous valuation and reflects how quickly AI-generated content is moving from novelty to business model. Suno's success comes as the music industry grapples with AI's creative potential versus copyright concerns. The valuation suggests investors believe we're past the "will people use AI music?" question and into "who will own the infrastructure?" territory.

**Anthropic goes ad-free, takes shot at Google**

Anthropic [announced Claude will remain permanently ad-free](https://www.anthropic.com/news), explicitly calling out how "advertising incentives are incompatible with a genuinely helpful AI assistant." The timing isn't subtle — Google just faced backlash for Chrome silently installing AI models, and Meta continues pushing ad-supported AI features. Anthropic is betting that enterprise and subscription revenue can scale without the privacy compromises that come with advertising models. It's a clear differentiation play, but also a constraint: they need to prove sustainable unit economics without the ad revenue that subsidizes most consumer AI.

### 📡 Quick Hits

**OpenAI brings models to AWS** — [OpenAI is now offering its latest AI models and Codex coding agent on Amazon's cloud platform](https://www.reuters.com/technology/openai/), expanding beyond Azure. Smart move to reduce Microsoft dependency and chase enterprise customers already committed to AWS infrastructure.

**Vercel ships DeepSec for security reviews** — Guillermo Rauch [announced an open-source agent orchestrator](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2051386798899888539) that runs deep security reviews against codebases. They tested it internally on major OSS projects before releasing. Could become essential infrastructure as AI agents write more production code.

**Chrome's stealth AI install** — [Google Chrome silently installed a 4GB AI model](https://www.thatprivacyguy.com/blog/chrome-silent-nano-install/) on users' devices without consent, sparking privacy backlash on HN. The model powers Chrome's AI features, but the lack of user notification is drawing regulatory attention.

**Chinese demand spikes Nvidia B300 prices** — [Strong AI computing demand in China nearly doubled prices for Nvidia's B300 servers](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/) to about $1M each. Shows how export restrictions create supply crunches that benefit secondary markets.
