**Grok Build Challenges Claude's Coding Dominance**

The AI coding wars just got interesting. X's Grok Build is drawing serious praise from developers who claim it's matching Claude's performance — a significant milestone for Elon Musk's xAI team.

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**Grok Build Emerges as Claude Code Competitor**

Multiple developers are reporting that X's [Grok Build coding assistant](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2059081339391869232) is performing at Claude-level quality, with one user noting they "spent a full coding session with Grok Build and honestly? It's right there with Claude." The praise focuses on both the model's intelligence and what developers are calling "the most beautiful (and performant) TUI" they've seen, featuring realtime context views and smooth agentic workflows.

This matters because Claude Code has dominated the AI coding space for months. If Grok Build delivers comparable results with a better interface, it could fragment the market that Anthropic has largely owned. Musk's retweets suggest xAI is betting heavily on this being their breakthrough consumer product.

**Vatican Issues Major AI Warning, Partners with Anthropic**

Pope Leo XIV released a sweeping 40,000-word encyclical warning about AI's potential to worsen inequality and automate warfare, while [announcing an unusual collaboration with Anthropic](https://x.com/washingtonpost/status/2059243218307301484). The document represents the Vatican's most comprehensive AI policy statement and signals religious institutions are moving from observation to active engagement with AI development.

The Anthropic partnership is particularly notable given the company's recent Pentagon blacklisting over autonomous weapons concerns. It suggests Anthropic is doubling down on ethical AI positioning even as competitors like OpenAI pursue defense contracts.

**Uber's $10M AI Coding Bill Raises Corporate Alarm**

A viral thread claims Uber [spent their entire AI budget on Claude Code subscriptions](https://x.com/TheGeorgePu/status/2059011818371100675) — 5,000 engineers at up to $2,000 monthly each — with unclear ROI measurement. While unverified, the story highlights a growing corporate concern: AI tool costs that scale unpredictably with usage, unlike fixed salary expenses.

This echoes broader anxiety about AI infrastructure costs hitting reality. Companies are discovering that "unlimited" AI access can generate unlimited bills, forcing new budget models and usage controls.
