## 🔥 The Big Stories

**OpenAI's First Custom Chip Is Real — and Broadcom Made It**

OpenAI has [unveiled its first custom AI chip](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/24/openai-unveils-its-first-custom-chip-built-by-broadcom/), built in partnership with Broadcom. This has been rumored for years — now it's shipping. The move follows a well-worn path: Google made TPUs, Amazon made Trainium and Inferentia, Meta made MTIA. OpenAI joining the custom silicon club signals they're serious about controlling their cost and performance stack long-term.

Why it matters: NVIDIA dependency is expensive. At OpenAI's inference scale, shaving even modest margins per token compounds into enormous savings. Custom silicon also lets you optimize specifically for your model architecture in ways off-the-shelf GPUs can't. This is infrastructure strategy, not a product launch.

Watch for: whether OpenAI opens this to Azure customers or keeps it internal. That decision shapes whether this becomes a competitive moat or just a cost optimization.

**Anthropic Says Alibaba Stole Claude's Capabilities**

[Anthropic is alleging that Alibaba illicitly extracted capabilities from Claude](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/anthropic-says-alibaba-illicitly-extracted-claude-ai-model-capabilities-2026-06-24/) — a significant accusation that lands at the intersection of AI IP law, US-China tech tensions, and model security. Details are still emerging, but "illicitly extracted" suggests something more systematic than standard API use.

This matters beyond the legal drama. It raises real questions about how frontier model capabilities can be protected — and whether terms of service are even enforceable at scale. Model distillation and capability extraction are well-documented techniques, and the line between "using an API" and "stealing IP" is genuinely unsettled law.

This will be one to watch closely as it develops. 868 comments on HN already.

**a16z Leads $200M Seed Round for Mirendil**

That's not a typo. [a16z is leading a $200M seed round](https://x.com/a16z/status/2069869327411749012) for Mirendil, a startup building frontier models specifically trained to do AI R&D — models that are expert at _building_ AI, not just using it. The pitch: frontier AI work has been locked inside a few big labs. Mirendil wants to democratize it.

CEO Behnam Neyshabur's framing is interesting: deep domain expertise that accumulates over time, like a senior scientist. At $200M seed, a16z is betting this "self-accelerating AI" concept is real and that Mirendil has the team to pull it off. Seed rounds at this size used to be Series B territory — the capital compression at the frontier is real.
