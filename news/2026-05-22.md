Quick Thursday reality check: while everyone's been arguing about model benchmarks, the infrastructure money is flowing fast. Brett Adcock's AI hardware startup just closed one of the biggest Series A rounds we've seen.

## 🔥 The Big Stories

**Hark Raises $700M Series A at $6B Valuation**

Brett Adcock's AI hardware startup [Hark completed a massive $700 million Series A](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-21/ai-hardware-startup-hark-valued-at-6-billion-in-new-funding-round) led by Parkway Venture Capital, valuing the company at $6 billion post-money. This is one of the largest Series A rounds in AI history.

The timing tells a story. As compute costs stay stubbornly high and chips remain constrained, investors are betting big on specialized AI hardware. Adcock previously founded Figure (humanoid robotics) and Archer Aviation, so he has a track record with complex hardware plays. The question is whether Hark can execute on custom silicon fast enough to matter in a market where NVIDIA still dominates.

**Modal Labs Closes $355M for Serverless AI Infrastructure**

[Modal Labs secured $355 million](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/21/serverless-ai-infrastructure-startup-modal-labs-seals-355m-funding-round/) to expand their serverless AI compute platform. Their pitch: developers shouldn't have to think about GPU clusters or container orchestration when running inference workloads.

This funding round signals investor confidence in the "infrastructure abstraction" thesis — that AI development will move toward higher-level platforms that hide the complexity of distributed compute. Modal's betting that most teams want to focus on their models and applications, not DevOps.

**DeepMind Ships Co-Scientist Multi-Agent Research System**

Google DeepMind [published research on Co-Scientist](https://deepmind.google/blog/co-scientist-a-multi-agent-ai-partner-to-accelerate-research/), a multi-agent AI system built with Gemini that can iterate on scientific research workflows. The system coordinates multiple specialized agents to design experiments, analyze data, and generate hypotheses.

What's notable: this isn't just another "AI can help with research" demo. The multi-agent architecture suggests DeepMind is serious about building AI systems that can handle complex, multi-step scientific workflows end-to-end.
