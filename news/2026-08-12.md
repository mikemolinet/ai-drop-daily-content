### 🔥 The Big Stories

**OpenAI's Ethics Chief Is Out**

OpenAI's head of ethics has left the company, [per the Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/e49dfb75-f841-4466-a577-f7aaff8779a0), less than a year after joining. The timing is notable: the departure lands against a backdrop of Sam Altman publicly thanking outgoing COO Brad Lightcap on X, suggesting more leadership churn than usual at the top. Ethics roles at frontier labs often get squeezed between safety researchers (who want to slow things down) and product teams (who don't). When the person in that seat leaves in under a year, it typically signals one of two things: the role lacked real authority, or the internal direction shifted in ways they couldn't sign off on. Either reading is worth paying attention to.

This is the second notable OpenAI departure signal in a week. It won't be the last. Watch whether Altman addresses it directly or lets it pass quietly.

* * *

**Researchers Are Stealing LLM Reasoning Traces — And It Works**

A paper called _[Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs](https://stolen-thoughts.com/)_ is the top story on Hacker News today (645 pts, 288 comments) — and for good reason. The core finding: hidden chain-of-thought reasoning in models like o3 and Claude Fable can be partially reconstructed from output patterns alone, even when labs deliberately suppress or hide the scratchpad. If you're building a product on top of a proprietary reasoning model and you think your inference pipeline is opaque, this paper says it might not be. The threat model matters for anyone deploying in sensitive verticals — legal, medical, finance.

The Hacker News thread is unusually technical and worth reading. Rohan Paul flagged related security concerns [on X](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2087512959941767644): clean your outputs, but your model's hidden reasoning may still leak. This one has real product implications.

### 💡 Builder's Corner

**LLMs are now better at writing specs than most humans.** Paras Chopra [makes a strong case](https://x.com/paraschopra/status/2087490594079928610) that modern models consistently surface requirements you'd have missed. Practical workflow: before writing a line of code on your next feature, prompt a frontier model to write a full spec. Review it for gaps, not correctness. You'll likely find two or three things you hadn't thought through.

**Mem0 hit 63K GitHub stars and is trending.** It addresses a real problem: instead of stuffing entire chat histories into context, [Mem0](https://x.com/N01ennn/status/2087511498989916365) extracts and stores relevant facts, cutting token usage by up to 90%. If you're building any persistent-user AI product, this architecture pattern is worth understanding.
