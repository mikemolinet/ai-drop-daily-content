The race to recursive AI improvement just got a timeline — and it's closer than most expected.

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**Anthropic Sets 60%+ Odds on AI Training Its Own Successor by 2028**

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark dropped a bombshell prediction this week: there's better than even odds that an AI model will fully train its successor by the end of 2028. This isn't just about code generation — Clark is talking about "early signs" of AI systems not just coding products but literally building themselves. The timeline is aggressive, but coming from a company that's seen the inside of frontier model development, it carries weight. If true, we're looking at the beginning of recursive self-improvement cycles that could accelerate AI progress beyond current exponential curves.

**Sam Altman Hints at New OpenAI Capabilities with Codex 5.5**

OpenAI's CEO is dropping breadcrumbs about improvements that go well beyond coding. Altman tweeted that "5.5 in codex is so good for non-coding tasks" and that he keeps being "pleasantly surprised" by capabilities he didn't expect. This suggests OpenAI's next-gen models are showing emergent abilities across domains — exactly the kind of generalization leap that could support Anthropic's 2028 prediction. The fact that Altman is publicly surprised by his own company's model performance indicates we're in uncharted capability territory.

**Developer Tool Companies Miss the Agent Opportunity**

A sharp observation from Anthropic's team: most developer tools are still marketing to developers when their products are now useful to anyone with access to an agent. The messaging remains "intimidating and unintelligible to normal" users, creating a massive market expansion opportunity. This isn't just about dumbing down interfaces — it's about recognizing that AI agents are democratizing technical capabilities. Companies that pivot their positioning first will capture markets they never knew they had.
