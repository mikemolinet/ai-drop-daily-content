**Apple Ships Gemini-Powered Siri as Microsoft AI Tools Hit by Security Breach**

Apple just dropped the biggest AI news in months, but a massive security incident is overshadowing the celebration.

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**Apple Reveals Gemini-Powered AI Architecture**

Apple finally showed its hand on AI, announcing a new architecture that integrates Google's Gemini models with on-device processing. The hybrid approach keeps simple requests local using a Gemma-like model, but routes complex queries to Gemini in the cloud. [The announcement](https://www.apple.com/apple-intelligence/) reveals Apple's bet on Google over OpenAI for its next-gen Siri, marking a significant shift from earlier speculation about ChatGPT integration.

What's interesting: Apple was notably light on technical details this time compared to previous releases. The on-device capabilities appear "extremely limited" without cloud backup, suggesting most real AI work still happens server-side. For developers, the new [Core AI Framework](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreai/) provides hooks into these capabilities, but expect tight Apple control over what gets access.

**Microsoft's AI Developer Tools Compromised in Supply Chain Attack**

Security researchers discovered that Microsoft's open-source development tools were hacked specifically to steal credentials from AI developers. The attack targeted the supply chain, embedding malicious code that harvested passwords and API keys from developers working on AI projects. [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/microsofts-open-source-tools-were-hacked-to-steal-passwords-of-ai-developers/) this appears to be a coordinated effort to access AI training data and model weights.

This hits at a critical moment when AI companies are increasingly protective of their intellectual property. The breach underscores how AI development has become a high-value target for both corporate espionage and nation-state actors. Microsoft is rolling out patches, but the incident raises questions about securing the AI development toolchain.

### 📡 Quick Hits

**Cognition Drops FrontierCode Benchmark, Models Struggle**

Cognition released FrontierCode, a new coding benchmark that's humbling even the best models. Claude Opus 4.8 managed just 13.4%, while GPT-5.5 scored 6.3% and Gemini 3.1 Pro hit 4.7%. The benchmark tests real-world coding scenarios that apparently expose significant gaps in current AI capabilities.

**China's Moonshot AI Targets $30B Valuation**

Moonshot AI is in talks for a massive $2 billion funding round that would value the ChatGPT competitor at $30 billion. [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/china-s-moonshot-ai-seeks-30-billion-value-in-new-funding-talks) this would be the startup's third major raise, signaling continued aggressive scaling in China's AI race despite global tensions.

**TCS Plans 50/50 Human-AI Workforce**

India's largest private employer dropped a bombshell: TCS Chairman said the company aims for equal numbers of AI agents and human employees in the future. This represents a dramatic shift for a company with hundreds of thousands of workers and signals how enterprise is thinking about AI integration at scale.

**Xiaomi Ships 1000 Token/Second Model**

Xiaomi's MiMo-v2.5-Pro-UltraSpeed claims to generate 1000 tokens per second, potentially setting a new speed benchmark for inference. If the claims hold up, this could pressure other providers on latency-sensitive applications where speed matters more than absolute quality.

### 💡 Builder's Corner

**ActiveGraph Framework Goes Live**

Yohei Nakajima released ActiveGraph concepts with an interactive learning site and open-source AG Coder, a reference implementation for coding agents. The framework promises better state management for multi-step AI workflows. Early, but worth tracking if you're building complex agent systems.

**LangChain's DeepAgents in 90 Seconds**

Harrison Chase demoed DeepAgents for competitive analysis, showing how quickly you can spin up research workflows. The speed of setup suggests the tooling is maturing for practical business intelligence use cases beyond just coding assistance.
