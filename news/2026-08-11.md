# AI News Drop

**Tuesday, August 11, 2026**

The theme today: open models are getting serious infrastructure money behind them — and the models themselves are getting harder to distinguish from "closed" ones.

* * *

### 🔥 The Big Stories

**IBM Signs $240M Deal With Together AI for Massive Open-Source Inference**

IBM is writing a nine-figure check to Together AI to deploy a large-scale inference cluster on IBM Cloud, powered by Nvidia B300 systems and Spectrum-X networking. Together AI will use it to push past 400 trillion tokens per month. That's not a pilot — that's production-scale infrastructure baked into a multi-year commitment.

Why it matters: Together has been quietly becoming the backbone for enterprises that want open-model inference without building the plumbing themselves. This deal signals that IBM — not exactly known for bleeding-edge bets — sees open-source model inference as the default enterprise path, not a side option. The B300 + Spectrum-X stack is also a meaningful endorsement of Nvidia's networking play at scale.

Watch for: whether this accelerates Together's position as the neutral "open inference" layer that big enterprises route through, the way Stripe became the neutral payment layer.

* * *

**OpenAI Ships GPT-5.6-Cyber — First Model Trained Directly on Defensive Security**

OpenAI [released GPT-5.6-Cyber](https://x.com/jxnlco/status/2086929308313141463), its first large-scale attempt at improving AI capabilities specifically for cybersecurity defense. Sam Altman amplified it with a direct ask: ["please consider using our models to help defend your systems."](https://x.com/sama/status/2086881528282587524) That's an unusually pointed pitch.

This is the first time OpenAI has explicitly trained a model variant toward defensive security as a primary capability — not just a feature. It matters because the attack/defense asymmetry in AI-assisted security has been a real concern: models that can write exploits were shipping faster than models optimized to catch them. A purpose-built defensive model, if it's actually good, changes that calculus.

What to watch: red team results. "Optimized for defense" is easy to claim. The community will stress-test it fast.

* * *

**Claude Now Adds Invisible Watermarks to EU-Deployed Output**

Anthropic [quietly shipped invisible watermarking](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content) for Claude models launched in the EU on or after August 2, 2026. The mark is embedded in generated text — undetectable to readers, but presumably machine-readable. [It's picking up traction on HN](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content) (219 pts) and catching developer attention.

The EU angle is the tell — this is almost certainly compliance-driven by the AI Act's content provenance requirements. But it sets a precedent: if Anthropic ships it for EU, expect it globally. The practical implication for builders is real: text your app generates via Claude API may now carry a hidden signal. If you're doing anything where AI authorship disclosure matters (legal, journalism, publishing), check the docs.

### 💡 Builder's Corner

**Swyx ran a head-to-head: GPT Luna Max vs. Claude Fable UltraCode** — same prompt, build a mostly-faithful clone of Grok Imagine using open models via fal.ai.
